1. What MVP is designed to learn

The MVP is intentionally focused on answering two questions about customer intent. The eventual goal of this capability is to create a new top-of-funnel lead generation channel for the web agent — contacts that downstream Microsoft sellers and marketing can engage with through follow-up activities. Before we build out the full pipeline (UCMP contact creation, marketing taxonomy, Email Agent follow-ups), we need to know whether customers actually want what we're offering and, if so, what level of follow-up they're open to.

The two signals we want to measure in MVP:
  1. Transcript opt-in rate — how many customers voluntarily share their name and email to receive a transcript, AI summary, and recommended next steps from their chat conversation.
  2. Marketing-comms opt-in rate — of those customers, how many also opt in to receive information, tips, and offers about Microsoft products and services — the signal that turns a transcript request into a marketable lead.

These two numbers together tell us whether the Business Opportunity Impact Estimate in the BRD (the sizing model projecting 900–5,400 monthly contacts and $2.2M–$13M annual pipeline; BRD Section 2.3 for details) is grounded in real customer behavior. They also tell us how aggressively to invest in the downstream pieces — UCMP integration is where these contacts actually become leads that sellers can act on, but that work only pays off if the upstream opt-in rates are healthy.

Two MVP design choices flow directly from this learning goal:

  • A customer-controlled entry point (Always-On button — see Section 3) so the opt-in signal is clean and unambiguously customer-initiated.
  • A minimal MVP surface area (no UCMP write, no marketing automation, no auth) so we can measure intent quickly, without confounding variables or long dependency chains.

2. MVP at a glance

Working from the latest BRD (W107) and our 5/27 sync with Jimmy and Chris, this is the MVP I'm working toward.

MVP in one line: A customer chatting with the Ask Microsoft web agent can click an Always-On "Get Transcript" button, share their name and email, and receive an email containing the verbatim transcript plus an AI-generated conversation summary and recommended next steps — with the option (independently) to also receive marketing communications from Microsoft.

At a scope level, MVP work splits into four areas. Section 4 shows how they connect end-to-end; this is what's in each.

Frontend — the Ask Microsoft chat surface

Everything the customer sees and interacts with:

  • Placement of the Always-On "Get Transcript" button on the chat surface
  • A soft nudge reminding customers the button is available anytime during the conversation
  • The inline, non-blocking contact capture card (name + email)
  • Two-part consent UI: transcript consent + marketing-comms opt-in, with country-specific defaults for the marketing opt-in (US opt-out default; non-US opt-in default)
  • Opt-in disclaimers and the privacy/transparency link
  • Coexistence with the Quoting flow: the Quoting experience already adds an "End quoting experience" button to the same chat-bar slot we plan to use for "Get Transcript." My current thinking: treat them as mutually exclusive — when the customer is in the quoting flow, "End quoting experience" takes the slot; outside it, "Get Transcript" is shown. Quote requests are infrequent enough that this trade-off seems reasonable, but worth a quick gut-check.

Backend — processing & handoff

Everything that happens once the customer submits the card:

  • Capture the details from the contact card and save the conversation transcript
  • PII scrubbing on the transcript and on the AI outputs, per CELA guidance
  • LLM generation (GPT-5 via Azure Foundry) of a conversation summary and recommended next steps — per Chris's prototyping approach, not just a summary
  • Hand off the assembled payload (transcript + summary + next steps + recipient info) to the email engine
  • Record the customer's consent choices (transcript consent + marketing opt-in) in our system so we have an audit trail of what each customer agreed to (note: these aren't yet passed through to CPM in MVP — that integration is beyond MVP)

Email delivery

The customer-facing artifact:

  • "Ask Microsoft"-branded format, tone, and layout
  • Body contains the summary and recommended next steps; full transcript follows
  • Assembled and sent by Claire's email engine (ACS)
  • Outbound-only for MVP: the customer receives the email but cannot reply and get a response back — inbound/reply handling is planned beyond MVP

Measurement & telemetry

What we need in place to actually call MVP a success or not:

  • Transcript opt-in rate, card completion, drop-off, country mix
  • Marketing-comms opt-in rate (conditional on transcript opt-in)
  • Reporting plumbing to read both signals from Section 1

3. The customer experience: entry point & consent

The customer experience is where the two MVP signals get generated, so the design choices here matter directly to what we can learn.

Entry point: Always-On button

Decision: MVP uses an Always-On "Get Transcript" button/link in the chat surface. The customer decides when (and whether) to invoke the transcript flow.

The Always-On entry point gives us a clean opt-in signal to measure (intent is unambiguous when the customer clicks the button), keeps the chat surface non-intrusive, and simplifies the CELA conversation.

Consent: two independent consents

Per privacy and CELA guidance, consent in the collection card is split into two separate, independent checkboxes — each tied to a distinct purpose. This split is already reflected in the current prototype.

ConsentPurpose / wordingDefault behavior
Consent 1 — Transcript email Permission to use the customer's email to send the chat transcript, AI summary, and recommended next steps. Required for the customer to receive the email. Customer must affirmatively consent to receive the transcript. (Without this, no email is sent.)
Consent 2 — Marketing comms "I would like information, tips, and offers about Microsoft products and services." Country-specific behavior. US: opt-out (pre-checked)  |  Non-US (GDPR markets): opt-in (unchecked)

The two consents are independent. A customer can give Consent 1 only (just wants the transcript, declines marketing comms) or give both. This structure is what lets us read the second signal in Section 1 — the conditional rate at which transcript-requesters also opt into marketing follow-up.

5. Phasing — what's intentionally deferred and why

MVP scope = everything detailed in Section 2. This section is about what we're holding back, and why.

Why this phasing: Holding UCMP and downstream integrations back lets us "light up MVP faster and start getting numbers sooner" (Srikar, 5/27). The MVP components are enough to produce the two opt-in signals we need; everything else becomes a follow-on investment once those signals justify it. We're intentionally not pre-bucketing Beyond MVP work into fast-follow vs. later phases yet — that should wait until we have MVP results in hand.

Beyond MVP (deferred)

ItemWhy it's not in MVP
UCMP contact creation/update + lead-source tagging (Web Agent) Turns captured contacts into trackable leads — the lead-gen outcome the BRD ultimately targets. Held back so MVP can ship without UCMP team dependency.
CPM consent write-through Consent choices are recorded in our system in MVP, just not pushed to CPM. Write-through pairs with UCMP enablement.
Email Agent reply / multi-turn follow-up MVP is outbound-only. Inbound/reply handling requires the Email Agent platform and is a meaningful additional surface.
UCMP Insights with marketing taxonomy Personalized follow-up and marketing automation depend on UCMP being live first.
Authenticated experiences MVP signals can be read from unauthenticated traffic; auth adds complexity without changing the learning goals.
